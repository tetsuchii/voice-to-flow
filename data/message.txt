Enlight Full Product Workshop

Participants:

Benjamin (Product Designer)

Anna (Product Manager)

Mark (Tech Lead)

Sophie (UX Researcher)

David (Bank Partner Representative)

1. Introduction

Anna (PM): Thanks for joining, everyone. Today we’re going to go end-to-end on Enlight — the Learn & Earn solution we’re embedding into the bank’s app. We’ll walk through the widget, onboarding, dashboard, activity types, and the points + rewards system. Then, we’ll dive deeper into how missions and quizzes actually work — the flows, the required screens, and the user journey.

David (Bank Rep): Perfect. What I’d like by the end is a clear picture of what users will actually see and do, so I can present this internally.

2. Widget & Onboarding

Benjamin (Designer): Enlight starts with a widget inside the banking app. Think of it as a card on the home dashboard: “Boost your knowledge, earn rewards.” It uses Enlight’s visual identity — light, brightness — but adapted to the bank’s colors so it feels native.

Sophie (UX Researcher): Context is important. The widget could appear after certain triggers: checking balances, finishing a transaction, or even when users explore savings. That makes it feel relevant, not intrusive.

David (Bank Rep): Exactly, it should never look like an external add-on.

Anna (PM): Once tapped, the widget takes users into the Enlight onboarding flow. Three lightweight screens:

Learn: “Enhance your financial skills.”

Earn: “Complete challenges to gain points.”

Redeem: “Exchange points for rewards.”

Each screen has illustrations and a clear CTA: “Get Started.”

Mark (Tech Lead): Technically, onboarding is simple. We’ll link the user’s Enlight profile via the bank’s SSO. That way, progress and points are tied securely to their banking identity.

3. Dashboard Overview

Benjamin (Designer): After onboarding, users land on the Enlight Dashboard, the hub of the experience. It includes:

Points balance at the top.

Progress tracker — e.g., “40% towards your next reward.”

Tabs or cards for Missions and Quizzes.

A “Rewards” tab where they can browse items.

Sophie (UX Researcher): Personalization is key. If someone just got their first credit card, the dashboard should surface a quiz like “How to use credit responsibly.”

David (Bank Rep): That makes the experience contextual and useful, not generic gamification.

4. Missions – Concept and Flow

Anna (PM): Let’s go deep into missions. These are real-world banking actions users must take to earn points.

Benjamin (Designer): I see missions as a 4-screen flow:

Mission List Screen:

Shows all available missions with titles, progress bars, and rewards.

Filter by Active, Completed, Upcoming.

Mission Detail Screen:

Full description, steps required, deadline, and points reward.

CTA: “Start Mission.”

Mission Progress Screen:

Live tracker: “1/3 deposits complete.”

Visual animations as progress grows.

Links back to the relevant banking flow (e.g., “Go to Bill Payments”).

Mission Completion Screen:

Celebration animation.

Badge unlocked (e.g., “Savings Explorer”).

Confirmation: “Mission Complete! You earned 150 points.”

Button: “Go to Rewards.”

Sophie (UX Researcher): Feedback at every step matters. Even partial progress should feel rewarding. “Nice! 2 deposits down, 1 to go.”

Mark (Tech Lead): Missions rely on event tracking from the bank’s system. We’ll need APIs to detect when the user completes a qualifying action.

David (Bank Rep): That’s powerful for us. It drives product adoption — more savings, more digital bill payments. But yes, clarity is essential: users must know exactly which actions count.

Benjamin (Designer): That’s why the detail screen has a “How this works” FAQ. No ambiguity.

5. Quizzes – Concept and Flow

Anna (PM): Now, quizzes. These are scenario-based learning experiences.

Benjamin (Designer): The 5-screen quiz journey looks like this:

Quiz Selection Screen:

Library of quizzes with themes: “Budgeting Basics,” “Travel Safety,” “Credit Cards 101.”

Cards show difficulty, estimated time, reward points.

Quiz Intro Screen:

Title, description, and learning outcome.

Reward points highlighted.

CTA: “Start Quiz.”

Question Screen (repeated):

Scenario at top.

Multiple-choice answers.

Feedback immediately after submission: correct answer glows, wrong answer dims with explanation.

Progress Screen:

Quick indicator: “Question 2 of 5.”

Option to exit and resume later.

Quiz Completion Screen:

Final score, points earned.

Knowledge recap (“You learned: Credit cards abroad may charge hidden fees”).

Enlight animation — glowing light or rays.

CTAs: “Retry for bonus points” or “Go to Rewards.”

Sophie (UX Researcher): Retakes are important. If someone only gets 2/5, they should still earn partial points, but full points unlock if they retry and ace it. That’s positive reinforcement.

Mark (Tech Lead): From backend perspective, we’ll track completion, attempts, and scoring. The quiz engine lives in Enlight’s system, embedded into the bank app.

David (Bank Rep): Could we tier quizzes? Beginner, intermediate, expert?

Anna (PM): Absolutely. That keeps advanced users engaged and allows progression.

6. Points, Progression, and Rewards

Anna (PM): Let’s revisit the points system.

Benjamin (Designer): Points are the core currency. Users always see their balance. To keep motivation high, we’ll add milestones and tiers — e.g., Tier 1 unlocks at 500 points, Tier 2 at 1,000.

Sophie (UX Researcher): Badges and identity matter too. Titles like “Quiz Master,” “Savings Starter.” They give status beyond rewards.

David (Bank Rep): And rewards?

Anna (PM): Three categories:

Financial perks: cashback, fee waivers, savings bonuses.

Lifestyle: gift cards, partner discounts, cinema tickets.

Charitable: donate points to causes.

Mark (Tech Lead): Rewards will need a catalog API. Some items fulfilled by the bank, others by third parties.

Benjamin (Designer): The catalog will look like a shop: filters, categories, each reward card showing points cost + redeem button.

7. Retention & Engagement

David (Bank Rep): One concern: how do we keep users coming back, not just once?

Sophie (UX Researcher): Regular nudges: “You’re only 50 points away from your next reward.” Or notifications about new missions.

Benjamin (Designer): Visual freshness matters too — seasonal quiz packs, limited-time rewards, themed missions.

Mark (Tech Lead): Notifications must be subtle, opt-in. Banks don’t want to overwhelm users.

Anna (PM): Agreed. The tone must be helpful, not pushy.

8. Wrap-Up

Anna (PM): To summarize the Enlight flow:

Widget → Onboarding → Dashboard.

Two activity types: Missions (real banking actions) and Quizzes (scenario learning).

Each has defined screens and flows with feedback and progression.

Points + tiers + badges motivate.

Rewards catalog provides tangible incentives.

Retention strategies keep engagement alive.

David (Bank Rep): This paints a full picture. With this, I can already pitch it internally as a structured, engaging, and bank-friendly learning solution.
